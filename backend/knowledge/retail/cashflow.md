## Definition

A liquor store is a cash-conversion machine. Money goes out to a distributor,
sits on a shelf as inventory, and comes back at the register with margin
attached. Cash flow problems in this trade are almost never a sales problem —
they are a **speed** problem. The shop is selling; it just isn't selling the
things it bought.

## Industry best practices

- Keep **60%+ of inventory value in stock that turns inside 12 weeks**.
- Pay attention to **distributor terms**. Thirty days on a product with sixteen
  weeks of cover means you pay for it three months before you sell it.
- **Stagger large buys.** Two half-orders a month apart cost the same and halve
  the cash exposure at any moment.
- Hold a **clearance budget of shelf space**, not just of money. An end cap
  permanently assigned to markdowns keeps the tail moving continuously instead
  of in an annual panic.

## Common mistakes

- **Judging the business by revenue alone.** A shop can grow sales and go broke
  simultaneously by funding the growth with deeper inventory.
- **Confusing retail value with cash.** $220,000 of slow stock at shelf price is
  perhaps $155,000 of money actually spent. The distinction matters when you are
  deciding what a clearance really recovers.
- **Taking every quarter-end deal.** Distributors push volume in the last two
  weeks of a quarter because their reps have targets. Your cash position does
  not care about their targets.

## Recommended actions

1. **This week** — list the ten positions holding the most cash and moving the
   slowest. That list is your working-capital problem in one page.
2. **This month** — cut or cancel reorders above 12 weeks of cover.
3. **This quarter** — phase a clearance sized to what the shop can absorb. A
   clearance worth two months of total revenue is a two-quarter programme, not a
   weekend sale.

## Benchmarks

- **60%+** of inventory value in stock turning inside 12 weeks
- Clearance realistically recovers **~60% of retail value**
- A clearance can add roughly **15% to a normal month's sales** before it starts
  cannibalising full-price trade

## Store tips

The most useful question when facing a slow position is not "how do I sell
this?" but **"what would I pay today to have this cash back?"** If the answer is
"a 30% discount", take the 30% discount. The bottle is not going to become more
valuable by waiting.

## AI notes

Always distinguish retail value from cash. Size any clearance recommendation
against the store's actual monthly revenue — a plan the shop cannot physically
absorb is not a plan. Give a timeframe in months, not "now".
