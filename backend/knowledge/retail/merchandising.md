## Definition

Merchandising is the cheapest lever in the shop. It changes what sells without
changing what you paid, what you charge, or who walks in.

## Industry best practices

- **Eye level is buy level.** The band from roughly 4.5 to 5.5 feet takes the
  most sales. Put margin there, not the cheapest facing.
- **The cold box drives the basket.** Chilled beer, RTD and white wine are
  destination purchases; everything between the door and the cold box is
  opportunity. Route customers past the categories you want to sell.
- **End caps sell 3–5x their shelf equivalent.** Rotate them every two to four
  weeks. A stale end cap is a dead one.
- **Checkout is for impulse under $20.** Miniatures, mixers, single-serve, small
  gift items. Nothing at the register should require a decision.
- **Group by occasion, not only by category.** A "Taco Night" bay with tequila,
  mixers and salt sells more than the same bottles filed alphabetically.
- **Premium needs air.** A crowded shelf reads as cheap. Faced-out bottles with
  space around them read as worth the money.

## Common mistakes

- **Filing by supplier.** Nobody shops by distributor.
- **Putting the clearance bay at the back.** Markdowns are impulse; hide them
  and they stay.
- **Leaving holiday displays up after the holiday.** A Christmas end cap on
  January 3rd tells customers the shop is not paying attention.
- **Over-signing.** Every shelf shouting means no shelf is heard. Three strong
  messages beat thirty.

## Recommended actions

1. Move your **top ten margin products to eye level** this week.
2. Build one **occasion display** aligned to the next holiday inside its window.
3. Put the **clearance bay within sight of the register**.
4. Rotate end caps on a fixed schedule so it does not depend on remembering.

## Seasonality

Summer: rosé, seltzer, tequila, entrance-facing and chilled. Autumn: whiskey,
red wine, warm-tone displays. December: gift packs, premium, ribbon and boxes,
front of store. January: clearance forward, premium back.

## KPIs

- Units per week on end-capped products vs their shelf baseline
- Basket size (units per transaction)
- Sell-through on occasion displays

## Store tips

The single highest-return merchandising move for most independents is a **shelf
talker with a reason**: "Staff pick — drinks like a $60 bottle". A price alone
competes on price. A reason competes on judgement, which is the one thing a
chain cannot copy.

## AI notes

Merchandising recommendations must name a specific product and a specific
location. "Improve your displays" is not advice. Never recommend displaying
something that is out of stock.
