## Definition

A liquor store lives on repeat trade. The occasional tourist pays for a bottle;
the regular pays for the lease. RFM — recency, frequency, monetary value — sorts
customers by how they actually behave rather than how much they once spent.

## Industry best practices

- **Win-back beats acquisition.** Someone who bought from you six months ago
  already knows where you are and what you carry. That is most of the cost of
  acquiring a customer, already paid.
- **Treat VIPs differently, not just cheaper.** Early access to an allocated
  bourbon is worth more to a regular than 10% off, and costs you nothing.
- **Ask at the register, once.** Phone number for restock alerts on allocated
  bottles is the highest-consent, highest-value data a shop can collect.
- **Segment before you send.** A message to everyone is a message to nobody, and
  it burns the opt-in list you spent a year building.
- **Respect opt-out immediately and visibly.** One ignored opt-out costs more in
  reputation than a year of campaigns earns.

## Common mistakes

- **Discounting to VIPs**, who would have bought at full price.
- **Messaging weekly.** The unsubscribe rate is the real cost, and it is
  permanent.
- **Treating "Inactive" as a lost cause.** Some are; some just moved house and
  need a reason to come back.
- **Collecting data with no plan to use it.** A list you never message is a
  liability, not an asset.

## Recommended actions

1. Pull the **At Risk** segment — recent enough to remember you, quiet enough to
   be drifting. That is the highest-return message in the shop.
2. Give them a **reason, not just a discount**: a new arrival in a category they
   bought before.
3. **VIPs get access**, not price: allocations, tastings, first refusal.
4. Track response by segment so the next campaign is better aimed.

## KPIs

- Repeat purchase rate
- Segment sizes and their movement between periods
- Response rate by segment
- Opt-in growth; opt-out rate per campaign

## Benchmarks

- Win-back response: **5–10%**
- Healthy repeat rate for a neighbourhood store: **40%+** of transactions
- Opt-out per campaign should stay under **1%**

## Store tips

The best retention tool in a liquor store is **remembering what someone drinks**.
A shop that says "the Rioja you liked came back in" does not need a loyalty
programme.

## AI notes

Always check segment counts before recommending a campaign. Never recommend
contacting a segment with zero customers. Distinguish measured segment sizes
from the assumed response rate — the first is his data, the second is ours.
