## Products

Premium spirits, gift packs, Champagne and sparkling, cream liqueurs, aged
whiskey and añejo tequila, wine gift sets. December is the month the shop sells
its most expensive bottles.

## Customer behaviour

The buyer is frequently not the drinker. That single fact drives everything:
packaging matters more than taste notes, price bands act as social signals ("a
$50 bottle" rather than "a good bourbon"), and the customer wants to be told
what is appropriate rather than what is best. Deliberation is high and price
sensitivity is unusually low.

The second wave — December 23rd and 24th — is pure panic buying. Whatever is
visible and giftable sells.

## Recommended promotions

Do **not** run percentage-off sales in December. You are discounting the one
month customers will pay full price. Instead: gift bundling, free gift wrap,
premium tastings, and clear price-band signage.

## Bundles

- Bottle + glassware, presented as one gift
- Three-miniature flight for the "I don't know what they like" buyer
- Champagne + chocolate

## Inventory planning and buying window

**Order gift packs in October.** They are allocated, and December re-orders are
rarely filled. Build premium stock through November. **Stop buying seasonal
packaging by December 10th** — anything unsold on the 26th is a twelve-month
problem, because holiday packaging cannot be sold in January.

## Expected demand

The highest-revenue month of the year, weighted heavily to the final ten days.
Premium and super-premium over-index dramatically.

## Display and social

Gift displays up by December 1st. Price-band signage — "Gifts under $30 / under
$50 / under $100" — is the single most effective December merchandising move,
because it answers the question the customer actually has. Down and cleared by
January 2nd.

## Supplier advice

Gift-pack allocations are set in early autumn. Ask about return terms on
seasonal packaging before you commit — some suppliers take it back, and that
changes how deep you can safely buy.

## Cross-selling

Gift bags, wrap, cards, glassware, mixers for holiday cocktails.

## AI notes

Never recommend discounting in December. Recommend bundling and presentation
instead. Flag seasonal packaging bought after mid-December as a January
liability. January is the right month to clear whatever is left — recommend it
then, not now.
