## Definition

A discount is a purchase of something: speed, footfall, or shelf space. It is
only a good purchase if you know which one you are buying and it was worth the
margin you paid for it.

## Industry best practices

- **Discount to move cash, not to move units.** A markdown on a fast seller buys
  units you already had.
- **Go deep enough to matter.** 10% off a $40 bottle is invisible. Below roughly
  15% a shopper does not change behaviour; you have simply donated margin.
- **Time-box every markdown.** "Until it's gone" becomes the new price and the
  product never returns to full margin.
- **Use a loss leader deliberately.** A known-price item (a major vodka, a case
  of a national beer) priced sharply pulls traffic that buys at full margin
  elsewhere in the basket. An unknown item priced sharply pulls nobody.
- **Phase deep clearances.** Worst first, deepest discount first. The tail gets
  progressively easier as the shelf empties.

## Common mistakes

- **Across-the-board percentage sales.** "20% off everything" discounts your
  best sellers to subsidise your worst.
- **Discounting the wrong end of a brand ladder.** Cutting the entry size trains
  customers down. Cut the slow premium expression instead and you trade them up.
- **Marking down too shallow, repeatedly.** Three 10% cuts over six months is a
  30% loss spread thin enough that nothing ever moves.
- **Forgetting the shelf.** A markdown with no shelf talker and no position
  change is a price change nobody sees.

## Recommended actions

1. Identify the **top 20 positions by cash tied up** with over 26 weeks of cover.
2. Set a **single deep markdown** — 25–40% depending on age — with an end date.
3. **Move them physically**: end cap, checkout, or a dedicated clearance bay.
4. Print shelf talkers showing **original and new price**. The comparison is the
   product.
5. Anything unsold after the window goes deeper or goes back to the distributor.

## Seasonality

January and February are the natural clearance months — trade is slow, gift
stock is stale, and shoppers are price-sensitive. The worst time to clear is
November: you are giving away margin in the one month customers would have paid
full price.

## KPIs

- Units moved per week during the markdown vs before
- Cash recovered vs retail value of the position
- Share of clearance stock still on hand at the window's end

## Benchmarks

- Meaningful discount threshold: **15%+**
- Typical clearance recovery: **~60% of retail value**
- Markdown window: **3–6 weeks**, then deeper or gone

## Store tips

Put the markdown bay near the register, not at the back. Clearance is an impulse
category — nobody comes in looking for it, but plenty of people add a $12 bottle
to a basket while waiting to pay.

## AI notes

Never recommend discounting a product with under 4 weeks of cover. Always give a
depth and a window. State the recovery rate as an assumption, and the cash tied
up as measured.
