## Definition

Turnover is how many times a year you sell and replace your average inventory.
It is the clearest single measure of whether a shop is a retailer or a warehouse
with a till.

Turnover = annualised revenue ÷ average inventory value. Both at retail, or both
at cost — never mixed.

## Industry best practices

- **4–6x is healthy** for a US independent. Chains run higher on narrower
  assortments; independents trade turns for range, which is a legitimate choice
  as long as it is a choice.
- **Turnover varies enormously by category.** Beer and RTD should turn 10x+.
  Fine wine may turn twice and still be right. Judge each category against
  itself, not against the shop average.
- **Improve turnover by buying less, not by selling more.** Sales growth is slow
  and uncertain; not placing an order is immediate and certain.

## Common mistakes

- **Chasing turnover into stock-outs.** Turnover measured on an empty shelf
  looks excellent right up until customers stop coming.
- **Comparing to chains.** Different model, different assortment, different
  buying power.
- **Reading turnover without reading the mix.** A shop can turn 5x overall while
  a third of its cash sleeps, if the fast half is fast enough.

## Recommended actions

1. Compute turnover **per category**, not just overall. That is where the
   problem is actually located.
2. On the worst category, split it: what is moving, what is not.
3. Cut buying on the slow half before touching price.

## Benchmarks

- Overall: **4–6x/year**
- Beer and RTD: **10x+**
- Spirits: **3–5x**
- Wine: **2–4x**
- Fine and allocated: **1–2x** and acceptable

## AI notes

Always translate turnover into cash language before quoting the ratio. "2.46x"
means nothing; "your stock is moving about half as fast as a healthy shop, which
is why $220,000 is sitting still" means something. State the 4–6x range as an
industry benchmark, not as this store's history.
