## Definition

A distributor deal trades cash and shelf space for a lower unit cost. It is a
good trade only when the product already sells at a rate that consumes the
quantity inside a sensible window.

## Industry best practices

- **Check the sales rate before the discount.** The only question that matters
  is: how long will this quantity take to sell? Anything past 12 weeks of cover
  is a cash trap wearing a discount.
- **Know the calendar.** Reps push hardest in the last two weeks of a quarter.
  That is when the terms are best — and when the pressure to over-buy is worst.
- **Negotiate depletion allowances and display support**, not just unit price.
  A free case, a branded display or a rep working your floor is often worth more
  than another two points.
- **Split large buys where possible.** Same price, half the cash exposure at any
  one moment.
- **Ask about returns.** Beer and RTD are dated; knowing your return terms
  before you buy 40 cases changes what you are willing to take.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying on unit price alone.** The saving is certain; the sale is not.
- **Taking a deal on a new product with no history.** You have no sales rate to
  divide by, so you cannot know your cover. Take a small quantity, prove it,
  then take the deal next quarter.
- **Deep-buying seasonal stock too late.** A Christmas gift-pack deal in
  mid-December is next December's inventory.
- **Letting the rep set the quantity.** He is optimising his number.

## Recommended actions

1. For any offered deal, compute **weeks of cover at the current sales rate**.
2. Under 12 weeks: take it. 12–20: take a smaller quantity. Over 20: decline, or
   take it only if it is a genuine seasonal build with a plan attached.
3. Check whether the cash is better spent on **restocking sold-out fast movers** —
   which is almost always the higher return.

## Benchmarks

- Deal acceptable up to roughly **12 weeks of cover**
- Quarter-end (March, June, September, December) has the best terms
- A deal on a product with no sales history: **maximum one case**

## Store tips

The best answer to a rep is often "what can you do on the things I actually
sell?" Reps have latitude and would rather discount a mover than push a dog.

## AI notes

Never evaluate a deal without checking current stock and sales rate. If a deal
would push cover past 12 weeks, say so plainly and give the number. Compare
against the alternative use of that cash — usually reordering stock-outs.
