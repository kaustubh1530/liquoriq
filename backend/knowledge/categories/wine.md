## Characteristics

The widest assortment and the slowest turns in the shop. Wine ties up more cash
per facing than any other category and is the most common home for sleeping
stock. It is also where knowledgeable service earns the most, because the
customer usually cannot choose alone.

## Buying behaviour

Two distinct customers. The **occasion buyer** wants something for tonight in a
price band, and takes a recommendation gratefully. The **enthusiast** shops by
region, producer and vintage, and buys deeper. The first is most of your volume;
the second is most of your margin.

## Profit profile

Best percentage margin in the store, worst turnover. That combination makes
buying discipline more important in wine than anywhere else — a bad wine buy
sits for years while looking respectable on the shelf.

## Seasonality

Rosé and crisp whites: May through August, and nearly dead after Labor Day —
buy tight and stop early. Big reds: October through February. Champagne and
sparkling: New Year's Eve dominates, with Valentine's second. Thanksgiving is
Pinot Noir, Beaujolais and Chardonnay. Gift season rewards presentation.

## Display strategy

By occasion and price band, not by country. "Under $15 weeknight", "Dinner party
$20–35", "Special occasion" sells far better than an aisle labelled France.
Shelf talkers with a one-line tasting note outperform ratings badges, because
the note sounds like a person.

## Cross-selling and pairing

Cheese, charcuterie, chocolate, glassware, preservation stoppers. Thanksgiving
and Christmas are the natural moments for a wine-plus-food bundle.

## Premium upgrade path

Supermarket brand → recognisable regional producer → single vineyard. The step
that works is the second: a customer buying a $12 Cabernet will try an $18 one
from a named appellation if someone tells them why.

## Typical customer

Broad. Skews slightly female on white and rosé, male on big reds. Highly
responsive to staff recommendation — more than any other category.

## Suggested promotions

Mixed-case discounts (buy 6, save 10%) move volume without devaluing individual
bottles. Tasting events convert exceptionally well. Seasonal bundles work.
Straight percentage-off sales on wine train customers to wait.

## Supplier notes

Vintage changes are a buying trap: last vintage becomes hard to sell the moment
the new one lands. Buy shallower as a vintage ages out. Reps discount closeouts
heavily — usually because the wine is aging out, not because it is a bargain.

## AI notes

Wine turning 2–4x is normal, not a failure — judge it against its own benchmark,
not the store average. Rosé bought after July is a next-summer problem, so flag
late rosé buying. Never recommend a specific vintage or producer the store's
data does not show it carries.
