## Characteristics

The margin engine of most American independents, and the category customers
research before they arrive. Bourbon must be 51%+ corn, aged in new charred oak
— sweeter, rounder, the default American pour. Rye is 51%+ rye: drier, peppery,
the cocktail drinker's choice. Scotch splits into blends (approachable, gift-
friendly) and single malts (regional, opinionated, higher ticket). Irish whiskey
is typically triple-distilled and smoother, and sells almost entirely on
occasion.

## Buying behaviour

Whiskey buyers are the most brand-loyal and the most information-hungry in the
shop. They read, they follow allocations, and they will drive past two stores
for a bottle. They also trade up more readily than any other category — a
bourbon drinker at $30 is a genuine candidate for $60, which is rarely true of a
vodka drinker.

## Profit profile

Strong. Premium expressions carry the best margin in the shop and are not price-
shopped the way a national vodka is. Allocated bottles are the exception: they
sell themselves and should never be discounted.

## Seasonality

Heavily weighted to Q4 — Father's Day and the whole of November–December are
gifting. Kentucky Derby lifts bourbon in May. January is the deadest month and
the right time to clear slow expressions.

## Display strategy

Vertical by brand so the ladder is visible: entry at hand height, premium at eye
level. Never file by distributor. Allocated bottles behind the counter or in a
locked case — scarcity displayed is scarcity sold.

## Cross-selling and pairing

Bitters, vermouth, cocktail cherries, glassware, decanters. An Old Fashioned kit
built beside the bourbon sells three items instead of one. Food: smoked meats,
aged cheddar, dark chocolate.

## Premium upgrade path

Well bourbon → named small batch → single barrel → allocated. The step that
converts most easily is small batch to single barrel, because the story is
concrete: one barrel, one flavour profile, a number on the label.

## Typical customer

35–65, predominantly male, buys the same thing repeatedly until given a specific
reason not to. Responds to staff recommendation more than to price.

## Suggested promotions

Store picks and single-barrel programmes. A shop-selected barrel is the single
strongest whiskey promotion available to an independent and cannot be copied by
a chain. Tasting events convert well. Bundling a bottle with glassware works for
gifting; discounting rarely does.

## Supplier notes

Allocation is relationship-driven. Consistent ordering across a supplier's range
— including the slower expressions — is what earns the allocated bottles. Reps
push seasonal gift packs early; buy them in October, not December.

## AI notes

Rye and bourbon are not interchangeable in a recommendation. Never recommend
discounting allocated stock. Whiskey is the strongest premiumisation candidate
in the shop, so upsell recommendations should start here.
