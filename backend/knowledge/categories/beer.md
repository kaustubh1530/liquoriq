## Characteristics

Highest volume, thinnest margin, fastest turns, and the only category in the
shop that genuinely spoils. Beer is a traffic driver: people come in for it and
buy other things while they are there. Seltzer and RTD have taken shelf from
both beer and spirits and behave like beer commercially — dated, seasonal,
volume-led.

## Buying behaviour

Habitual and price-aware. Beer customers know the price of a 12-pack of their
brand to the cent, which makes it the best loss-leader category and the worst
place to try to hold margin.

## Profit profile

Weakest percentage margin, strongest cash velocity. Beer earns its space through
turns and through the baskets it builds, not through margin per unit.

## Seasonality

The most seasonal category in the store. Memorial Day through Labor Day is the
peak — light lager, seltzer, and anything that fits in a cooler. Super Bowl and
July 4th are the two biggest single days. Oktoberfest lifts Märzen in September.
Craft slows sharply in winter apart from seasonal releases.

## Display strategy

Cold box is the destination — route customers past higher-margin categories to
reach it. Cases and 12-packs at floor level (they are heavy and people expect
them there). Singles and craft at eye level. Summer: build a display near the
entrance; nobody wants to walk to the back for a cooler run.

## Cross-selling and pairing

Ice, snacks, limes for Mexican lager, mixers for seltzer. The Super Bowl basket
is beer plus snacks plus ice, and the shop that displays all three together
sells all three.

## Premium upgrade path

Domestic light → import → local craft → limited release. The local craft step is
the one an independent wins on, because the story is regional and the chain
cannot tell it.

## Typical customer

Broad, high frequency, low deliberation. The single most reliable repeat
customer in the shop.

## Suggested promotions

Case discounts around holiday weekends. Mix-a-six is the strongest craft
promotion available — it lowers the risk of trying something unfamiliar, which
is the whole barrier in craft.

## Supplier notes

**Dating matters.** Beer is perishable and old craft is a real write-off, not
slow stock. Know your return terms before deep-buying. Distributors deliver beer
directly and frequently, which means cover requirements are much tighter than
spirits — 2–4 weeks, not 6–8.

## AI notes

Beer needs far tighter stock cover than spirits; do not apply the 6–8 week
guidance here. Slow-moving craft beer is a spoilage problem, not a clearance
problem — recommend moving it fast or returning it, not holding for a markdown.
Beer is the right category for a loss leader; premium spirits are not.
